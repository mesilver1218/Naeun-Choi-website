Cascaded motor control, planar arm kinematics, and simulation–hardware integration.

Overview

This course team project combined embedded feedback control of a physical DC-motor-driven 1-DOF arm with the kinematic and dynamic simulation of a 2-DOF planar robot arm. It required low-level timing, motor communication, coordinate transformations, and a software visualization interface to work together.

The implementation connected an ATmega128 microcontroller and Maxon DCX35L motor to cascaded feedback controllers, while a separate C++/MFC–ODE environment simulated 2-DOF arm behavior and supported switching between hardware and software modes.

Multi-rate embedded motor control connected to a physics-based robot arm simulator.

Method & Implementation

  • Multi-Rate Motor ControlImplemented cascaded position PD (2 Hz), velocity PI (20 Hz), and current PI (200 Hz) loops for an ATmega128-controlled Maxon DCX35L motor.
  • 2-DOF Simulation and KinematicsDeveloped forward/inverse kinematics and a C++/MFC interface with Open Dynamics Engine (ODE) for a 2-DOF arm.
  • Hardware–Simulation IntegrationSupported physical 1-DOF and simulated 2-DOF modes, including joint-limit checks and handling of negative or repeated-angle commands.

Validation & Context

Demonstrated feedback-controlled motion on the physical 1-DOF arm and interactive movement in the ODE-based 2-DOF simulator. The available videos cover initialization, simulated motion, commanded rotation, and behavior under externally applied force.

This experience reinforced how low-level dynamics, control loops, actuator constraints, and simulator integration affect the reliability of an otherwise simple arm system.
