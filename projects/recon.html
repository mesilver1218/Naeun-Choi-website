Overview

Active 3D reconstruction asks a robot to choose where to observe next as it incrementally builds a map of a previously unknown environment. Maximizing newly visible surface coverage alone can cause expensive detours and repeated visits to already observed viewpoints.

As an undergraduate researcher in the Robotics & A.I. Lab, I investigated an informative path-planning strategy combining voxel-based information gain with weighted accumulated travel cost. The aim was to balance the expected benefit of additional 3D observations against the cost of reaching candidate viewpoints.

Balancing information gain against motion cost during active 3D mapping.

Method & Implementation

  • Voxel-Based Information GainEstimated how much previously unexplored surface each candidate viewpoint could reveal in an active 3D reconstruction map.
  • Travel-Cost-Aware PlanningWeighted information gain against accumulated travel cost to discourage redundant revisits and inefficient viewpoint selection.
  • Simulation EvaluationCompared reconstructed coverage and error across five trials in a 40 × 40 × 12 m simulated urban scene at a 15-minute horizon.

Validation & Context

Across five simulation trials, reconstructed surface coverage after 15 minutes improved from 64.8% to 77.5%, while reported reconstruction error decreased from 0.20 m to 0.18 m under the evaluated conditions.

These measurements describe the specific simulated test environment, not a general guarantee about reconstruction quality in real-world deployments.
