Modular Human Following

Visual Intelligence Group · KIST

A modular perception, mapping, and navigation stack implemented for Unitree Go2 and validated in simulation and indoor real-world environments.
RGB-D / LiDAR→Perception & tracking→SLAM + planning→Go2 motion

Overview

I independently designed and implemented a modular person-following navigation system for the Unitree Go2 quadruped, connecting perception, target tracking, spatial mapping, goal generation, path planning, and physical motion. The pipeline was constructed from scratch and tested in NVIDIA Isaac Sim and indoor real-world environments.

The system deliberately separated perception and target-selection logic from the navigation and execution stack. This made it possible to replace individual vision or planning modules, diagnose failure modes, and later reuse SLAM, Nav2, and robot-control interfaces for learned VLA-based following.

From RGB-D human observations and LiDAR mapping to obstacle-aware quadruped navigation.

Method & Implementation

  • Person Tracking and 3D LocalizationCombined YOLO11m, ByteTrack, and PersonViT; used RGB-D foreground extraction, camera intrinsics, and TF to estimate a tracked person's robot-relative position.
  • Human-Following ControlGenerated navigation goals around a 2.0 m following distance: reverse below 1.9 m, stop within 1.9–2.1 m, and approach beyond 2.1 m, while correcting heading.
  • LiDAR-Based 2D SLAM and Nav2Projected Unitree 4D L1 LiDAR data to LaserScan for SLAM Toolbox and occupancy-grid mapping; refined scan binning from 1° to 0.25° and used Nav2 for obstacle-aware movement.
  • Onboard Deployment and CommunicationDistributed vision to Jetson AGX Thor and SLAM/Nav2 to Jetson Orin NX; resolved ROS 2 and Unitree SDK DDS conflicts through domain isolation.

Validation & Context

The complete modular perception–navigation pipeline was exercised in Isaac Sim and indoor tests on the physical Go2. Architecture and processing revisions reduced measured average end-to-end latency from 945.5 ms to 185.9 ms in the tested baseline configurations; this comparison combines several changes rather than isolating a single hardware optimization.

The system also clarified the boundary between recognizing a previously seen person and actively searching when a target is outside the camera view; the modular baseline cancels its navigation goal when the tracked person is lost.
