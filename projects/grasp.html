CAPSTONE DESIGN · JAN 2024 – JUN 2024

Reinforcement Learning-Based Pushing and Grasping Policy

DQN-based action selection for manipulating cluttered objects with a robotic arm.
RGB-D heightmap→Push / grasp Q-maps→Action selection→Robot execution

Overview

This capstone investigated robotic manipulation in cluttered environments, where grasping a desired object can fail because nearby objects obstruct the gripper. Rather than treating pushing and grasping as unrelated scripted actions, our team built a reinforcement-learning policy that could choose between moving an obstacle and attempting a grasp.

We trained the policy in a Gazebo environment and subsequently tested the learned approach on a physical Doosan M0609 6-DOF robot arm with a two-finger gripper. The policy was trained in simulation and evaluated in real-world robotic experiments, providing a concrete Sim-to-Real workflow.

Learning when to push and when to grasp in cluttered scenes.

Method & Implementation

  • RGB-D and DQN Action ValuesConstructed color/depth heightmaps and used separate pushing and grasping networks to evaluate pixel-wise Q-values across 16 action orientations.
  • Push and Grasp ActionsSelected between approximately 10 cm pushing primitives to clear clutter and grasping actions to retrieve objects.
  • Sim-to-Real ValidationTrained the policy in Gazebo and evaluated it on a physical Doosan M0609 6-DOF arm, including successful first-trial grasps of unseen objects.

Validation & Context

The policy was trained and evaluated in simulation, then exercised on a real robot in corresponding physical setups, demonstrating grasping behavior on objects not previously encountered during training. The gallery shows successful first-trial grasps of previously unseen objects (a butter-waffle package, a hairband, and another novel object), as well as a separate pushing example in a cluttered-object scene.

No unverified grasp-success percentage is claimed here; this public summary focuses on policy structure, the action-selection mechanism, and physical validation.
